Editing a variety's name or supplier updates it everywhere, including in trials
that are already complete. That is usually what you want for a correction, and
rarely what you want for a genuinely different variety.

You can edit from the web or from mobile; the fields are the same.

## Renaming when a number goes commercial

This is the common case, and it is a legitimate edit. `BP123` gets named *Ruby
Red*, and the plant has not changed — so the record should not fork.

**Keep the old identifier in the name.** Edit it to `Ruby Red (BP123)`, or
`Ruby Red - BP123`, rather than replacing the number outright. The whole field
is searchable, so:

- Anyone who still calls it BP123 finds it.
- Anyone who only ever knew *Ruby Red* finds it.
- Three seasons of evaluations stay attached to one variety instead of two.

Dropping the breeder number is the quiet way to lose your own history.

<Callout variant="warning">
  Do not rename an existing variety to stand in for a **different** one.
  Historical scores follow the name and would quietly attach to the wrong entry.
  Add a new variety instead — that is a different situation from a rename.
</Callout>

## Removing a variety

A variety that has never been used in a trial can be removed cleanly. One that
carries evaluation scores should be left in place — removing it takes its
history with it.

If it is simply out of your programme, set its **Variety Type** to *Dropped*
rather than deleting it. The record survives and it stops cluttering the lists
people pick from.

## If it is already a mess

Duplicates and mis-typed names happen, and they are fixable. Tidy them up
yourself where it is obvious, or [contact support](/contact) — merging two
entries that both carry evaluations is exactly the kind of thing worth handing
over rather than doing by hand.
