A trial covers one planting at one location. **Trial Input** in the sidebar — above
the catalogs, below the home screen — is where you create one, and the same form
is what you come back to when a detail changes.

Everything the form asks for has to already exist in
[Data Settings](/data-and-sync/data-settings). If a dropdown is empty, that is
what it is telling you.

## Evaluator and planting date

The **evaluator** defaults to whoever is signed in. It is not a formality — a
trial's tasks appear on that person's task list and nobody else's, and the trial
shows on their map and not on anyone else's. Getting it wrong is the usual
reason someone says their home screen is empty.

The **planting date** is what the rest of the season is measured from.

## Crop and farmer

**Crop** decides which traits show up when the varieties are evaluated, so it is
not a casual change later. **Farmer** is the grower whose ground you are on, and
it also determines who receives the trial's reports by email.

## Trial type

Six types: Screening, Plot, Slotting, Strip, Harvest Pass, and Nursery Drop.
The list is fixed — it is not something you configure in Data Settings.

## Location

**Zone** comes from the geographical hierarchy in Data Settings, and it is the
one location field you pick rather than type. It is the smallest, most specific
area you have defined.

**Ranch** and **Field** are free text and technically optional. Fill them in
anyway. They are how you find this trial again in a catalog of hundreds,
especially when you plant a lot of the same crop — "Rose Ranch / white barn",
or the California convention of ranch 11, field 2A entered as `11` and `2A`. If
the field has no formal name, describe it: *the field behind the white barn*
searches perfectly well.

## Coordinates

**Latitude** and **longitude** put the trial on the mobile trials map and power
**Open Map**, which hands the pin to Google Maps. There are two ways to fill
them in:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Capture them on site">
    Standing on the plot, press the GPS button and the app fills both fields
    from the device.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Or drop a pin and type them">
    Drop a pin in Google or Apple Maps and copy the coordinates across.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Typed coordinates want the decimal format — one to three digits, a point, then
the decimals. You do not need every digit; three decimal places puts you close
enough to find the block.

## Harvest date, chemical type, and notes

**Estimated harvest date** and **chemical type** — organic or conventional — are
optional, but both print on the trial map report, so filling them in saves
explaining later.

**Notes and instructions** is free text and genuinely open. In practice it is
where driving directions and site etiquette live: *always close the cattle gate
before entering*, *drive slow around the barn*, *irrigated 36 hours after
planting*. Write it for whoever has to cover this trial when you cannot.

## Save early

Saving creates the trial. Everything else attaches to it afterwards, so save as
soon as the details are in — if you lose signal, or lose the phone into a canal,
what you have typed is already on the cloud.

## What comes next

Saving the trial gives you four more sections on it:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Trial Varieties">
    The varieties you are comparing, including the check.
    See [Assign varieties to a trial](/trials/assign-varieties-to-a-trial).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trial Layout">
    Where each variety sits in the field.
    See [Trial layout and the plot map](/trials/trial-layout-and-plot-map).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trial Tasks">
    The field work to schedule against it.
    See [Manage tasks for a trial](/tasks/create-a-task).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reports & Emails">
    The trial map and evaluations report.
    See [Export and email trial reports](/trials/reports-and-emails).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout variant="tip">
  The form is long. A browser or tablet makes it far quicker to work through
  than a phone, even though the phone can do it — and the phone is the better
  tool for the parts you do standing in the field.
</Callout>
