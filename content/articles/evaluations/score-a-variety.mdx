An evaluation is recorded against one variety in one trial. Open the trial, go
to **Trial Varieties**, choose **Manage Trial Varieties**, and pick the variety
you are standing in front of — then **Evaluate Trial Variety**.

The form is the same on web and mobile.

## An evaluation is a work in progress

You do not have to finish in one pass. Answer the traits you can see today, save,
and come back. Nothing is submitted until you mark it complete.

At the top there is a dropdown that jumps from variety to variety without going
back to the list. If you have unsaved edits it will stop you and make you either
save or clear them first — deliberately, because the usual way to lose an
evaluation is to walk away from it mid-plot.

## The form, in order

<Steps>
  <Step title="Maturity date">
    The date this variety actually reached maturity. Every days-to-maturity
    figure elsewhere in the app is built from it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Traits">
    Choose **Manage Evaluation Traits** to score the traits attached to this
    crop. Each trait accepts the answers set up for it in Data Settings — a
    rating, or one of a fixed list of options.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Score">
    An overall 1 to 10 for the variety. This is the number that drives the
    averages in the variety catalog.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluation grade">
    Your verdict, and you may pick only one — see
    [Grades explained](/evaluations/grades-explained).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pros, cons, and conclusion">
    Free text, and required. This is what a reader of the report will actually
    read.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Photos">
    Take them from the camera or add from the gallery. They appear in the
    evaluations report and on the variety's page in the catalog.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark as Complete">
    Until you do, the evaluation stays in progress and does not appear in the
    [evaluation catalog](/evaluations/the-evaluation-catalog) or feed the
    variety catalog's charts.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the form insists on

Four fields are always required: **score**, **evaluation grade**, **planting
date**, and **maturity date**. So are **pros**, **cons**, and **conclusion**.

The single exception is the grade **Unable to Evaluate**, which drops the
maturity date and all three text fields to optional. Everything else has to be
filled in before the evaluation will save as complete.

That is a deliberate floor, not an oversight. You are spending a season and real
money on these trials; the pros, cons, and conclusion are how anything survives
the season in a form a supplier or a grower can read.

## Why the score runs to 10, not 5

Plenty of organizations grade one to five, and one to ten maps onto that
cleanly. The reason for the longer scale is that the distance between a seven
and a nine is a genuinely different judgement from the distance between a four
and a five, and a five-point scale flattens it.

In practice: a ten is gold in the field and should be rare, nines are unusual,
eights and sevens carry most of the good material, four and five are fair to
middling, and anything at three or below has a real defect behind it.

## Writing the text fields

Write as if you were messaging the supplier or the grower directly, because that
is roughly where it ends up. What looked good, what looked bad, what you are
going to do about it, and whether you would trial it again. "Great fruit",
"slow maturity", and "re-trial next year" is thin but honest; a sentence each is
better.

## If the trait list is empty

The traits shown come from the trial's crop. An empty list means no traits are
attached to that crop yet — see [Crop traits](/evaluations/crop-traits).

## AI analysis

Where it is available, the app can draft the pros, cons, and conclusion from the
traits and scores you have already entered. It fills the fields in for you to
edit; nothing is submitted on your behalf. Let it finish before switching to
another evaluation.

<Callout variant="tip">
  Score the check variety in the same visit. Every comparison downstream — the
  report, the catalog charts — reads against it, and a check scored a week later
  in different weather is worth less than one scored beside its neighbours.
</Callout>
